Who is your God, Where is She

Kat Bové

No one could have painted a Van Eyck before the development of oil paint, no one could have made a Courbet before the industrial revolution, and no one could have created a Dalí before the emergence of psychoanalysis. So it turns out that the art we call timeless is in reality always very timely. It is always the product of the technology and ideology in which it was baptised. So it is too with the work of Kat Bové.

Her work shamelessly imitates mirror selfies, references the Instagram interface and parodies influencer culture. It is made with materials such as fluorescent acrylic paint, graffiti and Posca markers. Her style draws on cartoons and advertising art. The hyper-contemporary iconography she uses will be as cryptic to future generations as that of Greek amphorae or Egyptian sarcophagi. In every possible way, Bové's art reflects the moment it was made. Nowhere is this clearer to me than in how the artist deals with one of the exhibition's central themes: femininity.
